# Verification of `components_review.md`

Date: 2026-09-28. Branch `feature/phase7-docs` at commit `4d9d424`. Every line
number below refers to that working tree. Probe scripts referenced as
`probes/NN_...` live in `design/components_review_probes/` and run with
`julia --project=. design/components_review_probes/<file>`.

## Method

Seven Claude Opus verifiers each took one part of the review: sections 1
through 7, plus one for the overall assessment, the component table, the
"preserve" list, the anchors, and the verification note. Each was told to try
to disprove the review's claims, to note where a literally true claim is
framed misleadingly, and to back every verdict with `file:line` evidence.
Where the review described a probe, the verifier rebuilt it.

What I checked personally, on top of the verifiers' work:

- Reran the section 3 probe (predicate call counts) and the section 4 probe
  (proof union). Both reproduce the review's numbers exactly.
- Reran the feasibility-limit probe behind side finding 1 and read its output.
- Grepped the `TargetList`, `Request`, `covers`, `classify` and
  `n_way_coverage` citations behind the section 2 and 5 corrections.
- Read the two `followups` docstrings behind section 4.
- Ran the full test suite once (see "Verification note" below).

Other verifier anchors were spot-checked, not exhaustively re-verified. No
repository file was changed except adding this report and the probes folder.

Verdict scale: TRUE, PARTIALLY TRUE, FALSE, OPINION (a judgment the code
cannot settle).

## On the review's suggested sequence

The review orders the work as small cleanup (sections 5, 6, 7), then the core refactor (2 and 1), then consumer cleanup (3 and 4), then engine consolidation. The evidence supports the cleanup ordering: sections 5, 6 and 7 are accurate and low risk, and 5 has more to delete than listed. It reorders the rest. Section 4 is the one place where documentation is currently false, and a docstring and display fix costs little. Section 1's substance is the negative sub-request projection, not a general layer. Section 2's substance is the duplicated target ordering and negative-target enumeration, not `Request`'s field list. Section 3 is sound but modest, and the discarded explanation lists are the larger cost. Engine consolidation would change every unconstrained design and the benchmark fingerprints, so the review's caution there is, if anything, too mild.
